\section{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

Novelty is not a property of an idea alone; it is a property of an idea read
against the work that preceded it. Evaluating it therefore requires three
things for every example: a target idea, the prior ideas that were available
when the target was proposed, and the contribution that survives those priors.
Assembling all three together is what \textsc{IdeaDelta} is for.

\subsection{Task and labels}
\label{sec:dataset-task}

The unit of annotation is a research \emph{idea}: a single innovation claim
extracted from a paper. A paper that introduces both a new objective and a new
architecture therefore contributes two ideas, annotated separately. For a
target idea $x$ published at time $t_x$, the admissible priors are
$\mathcal{P}_x=\{p_k\mid t_{p_k}<t_x\}$. This temporal constraint does real
work: an idea published after the target says nothing about the target's
novelty, however similar the two are.

\textsc{IdeaDelta} annotates each target at two levels. At the \emph{pair}
level, every target--prior pair receives one of four coverage levels---%
\textsc{not covering}, \textsc{related but not covering}, \textsc{partial
cover}, \textsc{large cover}---recording how much of the target that single
prior accounts for; a target's retained priors also come with a ranking, which
supplies the ordering a model needs when it reads evidence one prior at a time.
At the \emph{idea} level, the target receives an ordered novelty grade from
\textsc{low} to \textsc{high}. The two levels answer different questions: pair
labels say what each piece of evidence covers, the idea grade says what
survives all of it. A target can hold no strongly covering prior yet still
score low, when several partial covers together leave little behind.

The idea-level grade is not an overall impression layered on top of the pair
labels. We instead annotate four quantities per target and combine them by the
fixed rule
$\mathrm{raw}(x)=(1-C_x)\cdot\Delta_x\cdot E_x\cdot(1-\lambda S_x)$,
$\lambda=0.45$, where $C_x$ is how much of the target the retained priors jointly cover,
$\Delta_x$ the substantive contribution that remains, $E_x$ whether the
retrieved evidence suffices for a judgment, and $S_x$ how much of the claimed
difference is merely surface-level. Binning $\mathrm{raw}(x)$ yields the four
ordered grades. The rule lowers novelty for exactly four reasons, each
separately inspectable: prior work already covers the target, the remaining
difference is not substantive, the evidence is too thin to judge, or the
difference amounts to renaming.

\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:dataset-construction}

\paragraph{Paper selection and idea extraction.}
We collect arXiv papers in graph machine learning, computer vision, and time
series, preferring work that was subsequently published and, among that, work
at major venues. An LLM extracts innovation claims from each title, abstract,
and introduction. A second pass discards statements that make no substantive
research claim---open-source announcements, bare state-of-the-art assertions,
and engineering deliverables---so that later stages grade claims rather than
boilerplate.

\paragraph{Prior retrieval.}
For each target, nearest-neighbor search over idea embeddings returns candidate
priors published earlier, and an LLM adjudicates the uncertain similarity band.
A cross-type retrieval pass links a target to relevant prior work of a
different contribution type, which same-type retrieval alone would record as
isolated. The retained priors are ranked for each target, and every pair
stays within one research domain.

\paragraph{Annotation.}
A single judge, \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro}, annotates all stages. Pair coverage
follows a hybrid rule: clearly covering and clearly unrelated pairs take their
label directly, while the uncertain band goes to the judge, which reads the two
ideas' technical content. The judge then estimates, for each target, joint coverage, residual
contribution, evidence sufficiency, and surface-level difference, and
the novelty rule above converts these into the idea-level grade. The
labels are model-generated; we inspected samples from every stage.
Appendix~\ref{app:dataset} gives the full pipeline.

The result is $21{,}111$ annotated ideas and $92{,}876$ target--prior pairs
spanning 2019-01 to 2026-09, split by date rather than at random so that no
training target postdates an evaluation target;
Appendix~\ref{app:dataset} reports the composition per domain.
Because the labels are model-generated, we also check that they behave as
novelty labels should---responding to the retrieved evidence rather than to
target metadata alone, and falling when a prior already covers the target
contribution---and report those checks in Appendix~\ref{app:dataset-validity}.
