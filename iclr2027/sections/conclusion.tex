\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

EGRD grades an idea by what an explicit set of admissible priors leaves over,
estimating coverage at the level of semantic units and predicting the grade from
the residuals that survive alignment with the evidence. Supplying that evidence
rather than retrieving it is what lets the design be checked.
Permuting the priors returns the model to the majority class, so the grade is
not read off the target's phrasing; removing noisy-OR aggregation costs more
than any other component, so dispersed coverage is what the support matrix is
for; and the decomposition produces a representation in which the grade is
linearly separable before any head reads it. That last point is the substantive
difference from a prompted reader of the same priors, and adding stages to the
prompt does not close it. What remains open lies elsewhere
(App.~\ref{app:limitations}): on expert labels the ordering transfers while the
level does not --- a gap that, because the evidence is an argument rather than a
score, a reader can locate in the priors themselves.
