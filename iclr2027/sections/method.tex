\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

Given a target research idea and a set of ideas proposed before it, EGRD
assesses the target's novelty relative to the supplied evidence in three
stages (Figure~\ref{fig:framework}). Stage~A selects temporally admissible
priors and estimates how much of the target they already account for. Stage~B
decomposes the target against that evidence into two complementary components:
unit residuals capture semantic content with insufficient historical support,
and interaction residuals capture potentially novel combinations of those
units. Stage~C predicts an ordered novelty grade from \textsc{low} to
\textsc{high} from the residual representations. Throughout,
$[\cdot;\cdot]$ denotes concatenation, $\odot$ elementwise multiplication, $d$
the encoder dimension, $\sigma$ the sigmoid, $\varepsilon$ a small constant
added for numerical stability, and $\mathcal{V}$ the non-padding positions of
the target; padded priors are masked out.

\subsection{Stage A: Historical Evidence Inputs}
\label{sec:method-evidence}

Given a target idea $x$ with timestamp $t_x$, we restrict candidate priors to
those predating it, $\mathcal{P}_x=\{p_k\mid t_{p_k}<t_x\}$, and retain the
top-$K$ ranked candidates as the evidence set
$\mathcal{E}_x\subseteq\mathcal{P}_x$. We use $K=5$ in our experiments
and retain all candidates when fewer than $K$ are available.
Each retained prior $p_k$ carries a vector of pair coverage features
$\boldsymbol{f}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{11}$.

To estimate coverage across the evidence set, a cross-encoder
\citep{nogueira2019rerank} encodes each
target--prior pair and a multiple-instance model
\citep{ilse2018attentionmil} aggregates the pair representations through
attention pooling and maximum pooling. Concatenated with evidence-score
statistics, the pooled representations predict joint coverage $C_J$ and a
four-class coverage distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{J}$.
The retained evidence, pair features, and coverage predictions
serve as fixed inputs to Stages~B and~C, grounding the residual decomposition
and providing coverage information for novelty prediction.

We summarize the evidence set in a global context vector $\boldsymbol{z}_G$, a
learned projection of twenty-one scalars concatenated with embeddings of the
target's aspect and contribution type. The scalars comprise the scaled target
timestamp, ten pair-score statistics over $\mathcal{E}_x$, and ten
joint-coverage features. Apart from the timestamp and the facets,
$\boldsymbol{z}_G$ re-encodes the Stage~A outputs, and Stage~C consumes it as
a summary of what the evidence collectively establishes.
Appendix~\ref{app:method-evidence} lists the pair and context features and
specifies the pair-scoring rule and the joint model.

\subsection{Stage B: Evidence-Grounded Residual Decomposition}
\label{sec:method-decomposition}

Stage~B decomposes the target into semantic units and estimates their support
from the retained evidence, representing the unexplained contribution through
unit residuals for content with limited historical support and interaction
residuals for combinations with limited joint support from any single prior.

\paragraph{Semantic decomposition.}
A residual cross-encoder, initialized from the Stage~A model and trained
separately from it, produces a pooled representation $\boldsymbol{v}_k$ for
each target--prior pair, which we augment with projected pair coverage
features as $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_k=\boldsymbol{v}_k+f_P(\boldsymbol{f}_k)$.
Averaging the contextual token states at matching positions across valid pairs
yields a shared token bank $\{\boldsymbol{h}_j\}_{j=1}^{N}$ over the $N$ target
tokens. We then use $M$ learned queries $\boldsymbol{q}_m$ to softly assign
these tokens to semantic units \citep{carion2020detr,locatello2020slot}.
We use $M=8$ in our experiments. The queries compete for each token,
$B_{mj}=\operatorname{softmax}_{m}(\boldsymbol{q}_m^\top\boldsymbol{h}_j/\sqrt d)$,
and the resulting assignments are normalized over tokens within each unit,
$\alpha_{mj}=B_{mj}/(\sum_{j'\in\mathcal{V}}B_{mj'}+\varepsilon)$, giving
$\boldsymbol{u}_m=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_{mj}\boldsymbol{h}_j$. The units
have no predefined semantic roles. A learned activity score
$\phi_m=\sigma(\boldsymbol{w}_\phi^\top\boldsymbol{u}_m+b_\phi)$ controls each unit's
contribution, while an orthogonality penalty on the attention matrix
discourages redundant token assignments.

\paragraph{Evidence-aligned unit residuals.}
\label{sec:method-unit-residual}
A learned support matrix estimates how strongly each prior $p_k$ supports each
unit $\boldsymbol{u}_m$,
$A_{km}=\sigma(f_A([\boldsymbol{u}_m;\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_k;
\boldsymbol{u}_m\odot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_k;
|\boldsymbol{u}_m-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_k|;\boldsymbol{f}_k]))$, where
$f_A$ is an MLP. Support is estimated separately for every target--prior pair,
so different priors can account for different units. Over the
$n=|\mathcal{E}_x|$ retained priors, noisy-OR \citep{pearl1988probabilistic}
aggregates support into unit
coverage $c_m=1-\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-A_{km})$; its complement, weighted by unit
activity, defines the residual weight $r_m=\phi_m(1-c_m)$ and the unit residual
$\boldsymbol{z}_U=M^{-1}\sum_m r_m\boldsymbol{u}_m$. Noisy-OR lets several
partially relevant priors jointly cover a unit that none of them covers alone;
we use it as a differentiable aggregation rule, not as a claim that the priors
are independent sources of support
(Appendix~\ref{app:method-representations}). An active unit with little
historical support therefore receives a large residual weight.

Throughout Stage~B we preserve the magnitude of the residuals rather than
normalize it away. We divide $\boldsymbol{z}_U$ by the fixed number of units
$M$ rather than by $\sum_m r_m$, so that the pooled representation retains the
total residual mass; the supported content is pooled the same way, as
$\boldsymbol{z}_C=M^{-1}\sum_m \phi_mc_m\boldsymbol{u}_m$. To connect unit
coverage to supervision, a learned, activity-weighted pooling of $c_m$
reconstructs joint coverage $\widehat C_U$, supervised by annotated coverage
and the fixed Stage~A prediction. The support entries themselves are latent
strengths learned without unit-level alignment labels.

\paragraph{Interaction residuals.}
\label{sec:method-interaction}
Units that are individually covered may still form a new combination. For each
unit pair $i<j$, an MLP maps their concatenation, elementwise product, and
absolute difference to an interaction representation $\boldsymbol{t}_{ij}$,
and a sigmoid head predicts its strength $g_{ij}$. We measure historical
co-support by the largest product of the two units' support strengths within a
single prior, $\bar{A}_{ij}=\max_k A_{ki}A_{kj}$, which gives the interaction
residual weight $r_{ij}=g_{ij}(1-\bar{A}_{ij})$ and the pooled representation
$\boldsymbol{z}_I=\sum_{i<j}\eta_{ij}r_{ij}\boldsymbol{t}_{ij}$, where the
learned attention $\eta_{ij}$ includes a $\log(r_{ij}+\varepsilon)$ bias that
favors pairs with larger residual weights. When different priors support the
two units but no single prior strongly supports both, their unit residuals can
be small while their interaction residual remains large, provided that
$g_{ij}$ is high. Co-support is only a proxy for evidence that the combination already exists: a prior that supports both units need not cover the specific
way the target combines them. The pooling keeps the residual magnitude here
as well: $r_{ij}$ multiplies $\boldsymbol{t}_{ij}$ after the attention
weights are normalized. An auxiliary classifier on $\boldsymbol{z}_I$ is
trained with idea-level labels for nontrivial combinations
(Appendix~\ref{app:method-representations}).

\subsection{Stage C: Novelty Prediction}
\label{sec:method-prediction}

Stage~C predicts an ordered novelty grade from the residual representations
and the evidence context. Because evidence availability and uncertainty vary
across targets, a learned gate $\gamma$ scales the global context within the
residual branch: the gate reads $\boldsymbol{z}_U$, $\boldsymbol{z}_I$,
$\boldsymbol{z}_G$ and the entropy of the Stage~A coverage prediction, and
fusion yields
$\boldsymbol{z}_R=f_R([\boldsymbol{z}_U;\boldsymbol{z}_I;\gamma\boldsymbol{z}_G])$.
Layer normalization over the fused residual, the covered content, the target
representation $\hbar_x$, and the context then gives the shared representation
$\boldsymbol{h}=\operatorname{LayerNorm}([\boldsymbol{z}_R;\boldsymbol{z}_C;
\hbar_x;\boldsymbol{z}_G])$. Since $\boldsymbol{z}_G$ also enters
$\boldsymbol{h}$ directly, the gate controls context only within residual
fusion.

Auxiliary heads predict substantive difference, surface change, evidence
sufficiency, and residual type. These predictions, together with
$\boldsymbol{h}$ and joint coverage, feed three novelty branches: a direct
linear classifier, a cumulative ordinal head that models grade order
\citep{cao2020coral}, and a
two-layer MLP classifier. Each branch produces a four-class distribution, and
their convex mixture determines an initial grade.

\textsc{moderate} and \textsc{high} account for most of the confusion between
grades, so a moderate-to-high refinement revisits that boundary once the
initial grade is set. The refinement upgrades an initial \textsc{moderate}
prediction when a weighted combination of the direct and ordinal
\textsc{high}-class probabilities and a separate binary-head score exceeds a
threshold. We select
the mixture weights and the refinement threshold on development data and fix
them for testing (Appendix~\ref{app:method-heads}). A separate continuous
novelty head supplies auxiliary supervision during training and stays out of
the inference-time grading rule.

\paragraph{Training.}
We initialize the residual encoder from the Stage~A joint-coverage checkpoint
and jointly optimize it with the decomposition and prediction modules, while
keeping the Stage~A models fixed. The objective combines classification of the
novelty grade and the residual type, smooth-$L_1$ supervision of five
annotated scalars, and regularization of the decomposition against redundant
token assignments, unused units, and coinciding unit coverages. The five
scalars are substantive difference, surface change, evidence sufficiency,
the reconstructed coverage $\widehat C_U$, and a continuous novelty score. Because level
losses alone leave the scalar heads free to settle on the median of their
target, each is also supervised in rank; the coverage term additionally
penalizes disagreement with the fixed Stage~A prediction, weighted down where
that prediction has higher entropy.
Appendix~\ref{app:method-training} states the full objective, defines each
term, and reports the coefficients.
