\subsection{Historical coverage models}
\label{app:method-evidence}

\paragraph{Pair coverage and evidence selection.}
For each target--prior pair $(x,p_k)$, the pair coverage model estimates how
much of the target the prior accounts for. A cross-encoder predicts
relatedness $a_k$, coverage conditional on relatedness $b_k$, and large
coverage conditional on coverage $d_k$, which factorize into a distribution
over four ordered classes---unrelated, related but uncovered, partially
covered, and largely covered:
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{\mathrm{pair}}_k
 = [1-a_k,\;a_k(1-b_k),\;a_kb_k(1-d_k),\;a_kb_kd_k].
 \label{eq:pair-probs}
\end{equation}
Indexing the classes from $0$ to $3$, the model scores the pair as
$s_k=\tfrac12\pi^{\mathrm{pair}}_{k,2}+\pi^{\mathrm{pair}}_{k,3}$, counting
partial coverage at half the weight of large coverage. The pipeline ranks
temporally admissible candidates by $s_k+0.02\ell_k/3$, where $\ell_k$ is the
predicted ordinal coverage label, and retains at most $K$ of them. The
retained priors and their pair coverage features form the evidence inputs to
the subsequent stages.

\paragraph{Pair feature vector.}
The features carried into Stages~B and~C are
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{f}_k=\bigl[\,s_k;\;s_k+0.02\ell_k/3;\;
 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{\mathrm{pair}}_k;\;a_k;\;a_kb_k;\;d_k;\;
 r_k/K;\;\mathbb{1}[\text{prior text available}]\,\bigr]
 \in\mathbb{R}^{11},
\end{equation}
where $r_k$ is the rank of the prior within $\mathcal{E}_x$. Through
Eq.~(\ref{eq:pair-probs}), the first seven entries are deterministic functions
of $(a_k,b_k,d_k)$, so $\boldsymbol{f}_k$ carries three degrees of freedom
from the pair model alongside the two ranking quantities and the availability
flag. We keep the redundant parameterization because the downstream
projections consume these quantities at different scales.

\paragraph{Joint coverage.}
Different priors may cover different parts of the target, so a single pair
score understates what the evidence establishes together. We therefore
estimate joint coverage with a multiple-instance model that treats each pair
$(x,p_k)$ as an instance and the retained evidence set as a bag
\citep{ilse2018attentionmil}. Let $n=|\mathcal{E}_x|$ denote the number of
retained priors. A shared cross-encoder maps each pair to a representation
$\boldsymbol{h}^{J}_k$, from which the model computes attention weights
$\beta_k$ and internal evidence scores $e_k$:
\begin{equation}
 \beta_k=\operatorname{softmax}_{k}(\boldsymbol{w}_a^\top\boldsymbol{h}^{J}_k+b_a),
 \qquad e_k=\sigma(\boldsymbol{w}_e^\top\boldsymbol{h}^{J}_k+b_e).
\end{equation}
For a nonempty evidence set, noisy-OR pools the evidence scores as
$e_{\cup}=1-\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-e_k)$. Four statistics summarize the scores---
that aggregate, their maximum and mean, and the fraction of the $K$ evidence
slots that are filled---and join the attention-pooled and elementwise
maximum-pooled pair representations:
\begin{align}
 \boldsymbol{v}_E
 &=\left[e_{\cup},\;
          \max_k e_k,\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_k,\;\frac{n}{K}\right],
 \\
 \boldsymbol{z}_J
 &=f_J\left(\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_k\boldsymbol{h}^{J}_k;
              \max_{k}\boldsymbol{h}^{J}_k;
              \boldsymbol{v}_E\right]\right),
\end{align}
where $f_J$ is an MLP. Attention pooling aggregates evidence across priors,
while maximum pooling retains the strongest feature values; the noisy-OR
aggregate enters only as one of the four statistics, and the prediction itself
is learned from the full bag representation. A hierarchical head maps
$\boldsymbol{z}_J$ to three probabilities: any coverage, substantial coverage
conditional on any coverage, and mostly covered conditional on substantial
coverage. Under the same factorization as
Eq.~(\ref{eq:pair-probs}), these probabilities yield $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{J}$
over four joint-coverage classes mirroring the pair classes: \emph{not},
\emph{weakly}, \emph{partially}, and \emph{mostly} covered. The scalar
estimate is $C_J=\sum_{\ell=0}^{3}\mu_\ell\pi^J_\ell$, where $\mu_\ell$ is the
mean annotated coverage score for class $\ell$ on the training split. Both
$C_J$ and $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{J}$ are supplied as fixed inputs to the residual
and novelty modules.

\paragraph{Global context features.}
The twenty-one scalars projected into $\boldsymbol{z}_G$ are the scaled target
timestamp, ten pair-score statistics, and ten joint-coverage features. The
pair-score statistics over $\mathcal{E}_x$ are the fraction of the $K$
evidence slots that are filled; the maximum, mean, standard deviation and sum
of the $s_k$; a union score over them; the fractions of retained priors judged
covering and judged related; the margin between the top two ranking scores;
and the entropy of the pair scores. The joint-coverage features are two
scalar coverage estimates, the raw and normalized entropies of
$\boldsymbol{\pi}^{J}$, an indicator for whether a joint prediction is
available, the gap between the joint estimate and the pair-score union, and
$\boldsymbol{\pi}^{J}$ itself.

\subsection{Residual representations and pooling}
\label{app:method-representations}

This section specifies the encoders, pooling rules, and masking behavior that
Section~\ref{sec:method-decomposition} leaves implicit.

\paragraph{Pair encoding.}
The residual cross-encoder shares parameters across all retained priors. For
each target--prior pair $(x,p_k)$ it produces token states
$\boldsymbol{H}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}$, where $L$ is the input sequence
length. Masked mean pooling over these states gives the pair representation,
which we augment with projected pair coverage features:
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{v}_k=\operatorname{MeanPool}(\boldsymbol{H}_k),
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_k=\boldsymbol{v}_k+f_P(\boldsymbol{f}_k),
\end{equation}
where $f_P$ consists of a linear map, layer normalization, GELU, and dropout.
The target token bank averages target-token states at matching positions
across valid pairs, and masked mean pooling over the bank yields the target
representation $\hbar_x$ that Stage~C fuses.

\paragraph{Coverage reconstruction.}
To supervise the unit coverage estimates, we aggregate them into a scalar
joint-coverage prediction. The pooling weights depend on both the semantic
representation and the activity of each unit:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat C_U=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m c_m,\qquad
 \omega_m=\frac{\phi_m\exp(\boldsymbol{w}_c^\top\boldsymbol{u}_m+b_c)}
 {\sum_{r=1}^{M}\phi_r\exp(\boldsymbol{w}_c^\top\boldsymbol{u}_r+b_c)}.
 \label{eq:reconstructed-coverage}
\end{equation}
Annotated coverage and the fixed Stage~A prediction supervise the resulting
estimate $\widehat C_U$. This supervision is aggregate: it constrains the unit
coverages without determining the latent support matrix uniquely. Noisy-OR
likewise serves as a differentiable rule for aggregating support, and using it
does not establish that support from different priors is statistically
independent.

\paragraph{Interaction encoding and attention.}
For each unit pair $i<j$, an MLP $f_I$ encodes the two units and their
elementwise comparisons into an interaction representation. A sigmoid head
then predicts the interaction strength:
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{t}_{ij}=f_I([\boldsymbol{u}_i;\boldsymbol{u}_j;
 \boldsymbol{u}_i\odot\boldsymbol{u}_j;|\boldsymbol{u}_i-\boldsymbol{u}_j|]),
 \qquad g_{ij}=\sigma(\boldsymbol{w}_g^\top\boldsymbol{t}_{ij}+b_g).
\end{equation}
Using the interaction residual weights $r_{ij}$ defined in Stage~B, we
compute attention over all $\binom{M}{2}$ unit pairs:
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}_{i<j}
 \left(\boldsymbol{w}_I^\top\boldsymbol{t}_{ij}+b_I+
 \log(r_{ij}+\varepsilon)\right),\qquad
 \boldsymbol{z}_I=\sum_{i<j}\eta_{ij}r_{ij}\boldsymbol{t}_{ij}.
 \label{eq:interaction-pooling}
\end{equation}
The logarithmic bias directs attention toward pairs with larger residual
weights. Multiplying by $r_{ij}$ after normalization then restores their
scale, so that weak residuals contribute less than the softmax alone would
allow---the same reason $\boldsymbol{z}_U$ is normalized by $M$ rather than by
$\sum_m r_m$.

\paragraph{Masking and edge cases.}
Padded evidence slots carry zero support: the evidence-availability flag masks
the support matrix, which is clamped to $[0,0.995]$ before use. Both
aggregations remain well defined when fewer than $K$ priors are retained. The
noisy-OR product runs over the retained priors only, while the co-support
maximum is taken over all $K$ slots, so $\bar{A}_{ij}=0$ when none is
retained---an empty record yields no evidence that the combination exists.

\subsection{Residual fusion, prediction heads, and decision rules}
\label{app:method-heads}

This section specifies the gate, the heads, and the decision rules that
Section~\ref{sec:method-prediction} states only in outline.

\paragraph{Context gate.}
The fusion gate conditions the contribution of global context on the two
residual representations and the uncertainty of the joint-coverage
prediction. Let $H_J$ denote the normalized entropy of
$\boldsymbol{\pi}^{J}$. The gate is
\begin{equation}
 \gamma=\sigma\left(f_\gamma([
 \boldsymbol{z}_U;\boldsymbol{z}_I;\boldsymbol{z}_G;H_J])\right).
\end{equation}
Here $f_\gamma$ is a two-layer MLP with a $d/2$ bottleneck and a scalar
output, so $\gamma$ scales $\boldsymbol{z}_G$ uniformly rather than gating it
per dimension. The gated context $\gamma\boldsymbol{z}_G$ enters residual
fusion alongside $\boldsymbol{z}_U$ and $\boldsymbol{z}_I$, while
$\boldsymbol{z}_G$ reaches the shared representation---and through it the
prediction heads---on an ungated path of its own.

\paragraph{Auxiliary predictions.}
The auxiliary heads use the shared representation $\boldsymbol{h}$ to predict
substantive difference $\widehat d$, surface change $\widehat s$, evidence
sufficiency $\widehat e$, and residual-type probabilities
$\boldsymbol{\pi}^{T}$. The first three outputs are sigmoid scores; the type
prediction uses the hierarchy described below.

\paragraph{Residual types.}
The type hierarchy distinguishes a minor variant from six substantive families:
conceptual innovation, mechanism or system, task or application, training or
evaluation, data-related innovation, and combination. Conditional fine heads
yield eleven types in total. The minor/substantive gate additionally reads
$\widehat d$, $\widehat s$, and $\max_{i<j}r_{ij}$. The probability assigned
to the combination type is multiplied by
$0.70+0.30\sigma(f_{\mathrm{comb}}(\boldsymbol{z}_I))$ before the hierarchical
distribution is renormalized. The final type distribution is a convex mixture
of the hierarchical and flat predictions, with flat weight $0.15$.

\paragraph{Novelty branches.}
All novelty branches receive the prediction features
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{\xi}=[\boldsymbol{h};\widehat d;\widehat s;\widehat e;
                    \boldsymbol{\pi}^{T};C_J;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{J}].
\end{equation}
We encode \textsc{low}, \textsc{weak}, \textsc{moderate}, and \textsc{high}
as $0$, $1$, $2$, and $3$, respectively. A direct linear classifier and a
two-layer MLP classifier map $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ to class distributions
$\boldsymbol{p}^{D}$ and $\boldsymbol{p}^{R}$.

The ordinal branch models cumulative probabilities of reaching each grade
threshold. It produces three sigmoid gates $g_1,g_2,g_3$ from
$\boldsymbol{\xi}$ and defines
\begin{equation}
 q_1=g_1,\quad q_2=g_1g_2,\quad q_3=g_1g_2g_3,
 \qquad \boldsymbol{p}^{O}=[1-q_1,\;q_1-q_2,\;q_2-q_3,\;q_3].
\end{equation}
Here $q_\ell$ estimates the probability that $y\geq\ell$, with
$q_1\geq q_2\geq q_3$ by construction.

\paragraph{Probability fusion.}
We aggregate the three branch distributions through a convex mixture:
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{p}=\lambda_D\boldsymbol{p}^{D}
               +\lambda_O\boldsymbol{p}^{O}
               +\lambda_R\boldsymbol{p}^{R},\qquad
 \lambda_D+\lambda_O+\lambda_R=1,
 \quad \lambda_D,\lambda_O,\lambda_R\geq0.
 \label{eq:novelty-fusion}
\end{equation}
The fused distribution provides the initial grade for the refinement below.

\paragraph{Moderate-to-high refinement.}
A separate binary head $h_{MH}=\sigma(f_{MH}(\boldsymbol{\xi}))$ scores
the distinction between moderate and high novelty. At inference, let
$y_0=\arg\max_\ell p_\ell$ for the mixture in
Eq.~(\ref{eq:novelty-fusion}), and define
\begin{equation}
 h_{\mathrm{high}}=\alpha_Dp^D_3+\alpha_Op^O_3+\alpha_{MH}h_{MH},\qquad
 \widehat y=
 \begin{cases}
 3,&y_0=2\ \text{and}\ h_{\mathrm{high}}\geq\theta_{MH},\\
 y_0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_D,\alpha_O,\alpha_{MH}\geq0$ and
$\alpha_D+\alpha_O+\alpha_{MH}=1$. Thresholding the weighted score, rather
than requiring separate agreement from all three heads, promotes a
\textsc{moderate} prediction to \textsc{high} whenever the heads are
collectively confident---even when each of them favors a different grade. We
select the mixture weights and the refinement threshold on development data
and fix them for testing; Appendix~\ref{app:method-training} reports the
values.

\paragraph{Continuous outputs.}
A separate sigmoid head predicts a continuous novelty score. Probability
fusion and the moderate-to-high refinement alone determine the grade at
inference, so this score never enters the decision rule.

\subsection{Training and implementation details}
\label{app:method-training}

This section defines the loss terms of the training objective and reports the
settings of the residual stage.

\paragraph{Objective.}
Let $\mathcal{A}=\{\Delta,S,E,C,\nu\}$ index the annotated scalars---the
substantive difference, surface change, and evidence sufficiency read off the
auxiliary heads, the reconstructed coverage $\widehat C_U$, and the continuous
novelty score. Each contributes a smooth-$L_1$ level loss $\mathcal{L}_a$
against its annotation, and the ranking terms of $\Delta$, $S$, $E$ and $C$
are collected into a single $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}}$, the novelty score
carrying its own inside $\mathcal{L}_{\nu}$:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{a}\mathcal{L}_{a}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{rank}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{nov}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nov}}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{type}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{type}}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{dec}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{var}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}},
 \label{eq:training-objective}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nov}}$ supervises the three novelty branches, their
fused distribution, and the moderate-to-high head;
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{type}}$ supervises residual categories and nontrivial
combinations; and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}}$ and
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}$ regularize the decomposition.
Each coefficient $\lambda$ weights its term in the sum.

\paragraph{Supervision.}
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nov}}$ trains the direct and MLP
classifiers with class-weighted cross-entropy, the ordinal outputs against
$\mathbf{1}[y\geq\ell]$, $\ell=1,2,3$, with weighted binary cross-entropy, and
the fused distribution with a negative log-likelihood. The moderate-to-high head
sees only moderate and high examples and adds a positive--negative margin to its
binary cross-entropy, weighting moderate examples scored as high more heavily.
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{type}}$ combines the hierarchy's conditional losses, a
flat classification loss, binary supervision for nontrivial combinations, and
consistency penalties tying the combination branch to the type predictions.
The novelty score regresses $\nu^*$, the continuous target that
$\mathrm{raw}(x)$ in the novelty rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset-task} forms
from the annotated coverage, substantive difference, evidence sufficiency and
surface change of the target, and that the dataset thresholds into the four
grades.

\paragraph{Scalar heads: level and ranking terms.}
Let $\ell_\delta$ denote smooth-$L_1$ loss and $w_x$ the example weight. For
$a\in\mathcal{A}$, the level loss is
$\mathcal{L}_a=\sum_xw_x\ell_\delta(\widehat a_x-a^*_x)/\sum_xw_x$, where
$a^*_x$ is the annotation and $\widehat a_x$ the prediction. The ranking term
takes within-minibatch pairs separated by $|a^*_x-a^*_{x'}|\geq0.05$ and
contributes
$[0.02-\operatorname{sgn}(a^*_x-a^*_{x'})(\widehat a_x-\widehat a_{x'})]_+$,
weighted by $\sqrt{w_xw_{x'}}$; it is dropped when a minibatch holds no
eligible pair.

Ranking is not redundant with the level loss. At the transition $\delta$ and
target spread used here the level loss operates in its $L_1$ regime, whose
optimum is the median of the target, so a head supervised by it alone drifts
towards a near-constant output---the evidence head most visibly. Ranking is
scale-free and counteracts that drift, which is also why it takes most of the
weight inside $\mathcal{L}_{\nu}$: thresholding reads the grade off the score,
so only order matters there.

\paragraph{Coverage, decomposition, and variance terms.}
Beyond its level loss against the annotated joint value $C^*$,
$\mathcal{L}_{C}$ adds a consistency loss against the upstream score $C_J$ at
relative weight $0.2$, reweighted by $1-H_J$ so that examples with uncertain
upstream coverage count for less; no unit-level support matrix is supplied as
a target. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}}$ combines three terms: attention
regularization penalizes the squared entries of
$\widetilde\alpha\widetilde\alpha^\top-I$ for the row-normalized attention
matrix $\widetilde\alpha$, plus half the mean off-diagonal cosine similarity;
query diversity penalizes the mean squared cosine similarity between distinct
queries; and a usage penalty
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}\|\boldsymbol{u})$, with
$\rho_m=\sum_jB_{mj}/\sum_{r,j}B_{rj}$ and $\boldsymbol{u}$ uniform over the
$M$ units, keeps units from going unused. The variance floor carries its own
coefficient,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}
 =\mathbb{E}_x\bigl[[\nu-\operatorname{Var}_m(c_{x,m})]_+/\nu\bigr],
\end{equation}
and acts on the unit coverages rather than on the token assignments, keeping
the $c_m$ within an example from coinciding.

\paragraph{Curriculum and feature perturbation.}
For a randomly sampled fraction of training examples, values derived from
annotated joint coverage replace the joint-coverage features and the
associated probability and entropy features---with probability $0.50$ in epoch
1, $0.20$ in epoch 2, and $0$ thereafter. Development and test runs always use
the upstream joint predictions.
Selected joint-coverage features on the global-context path also receive
Gaussian noise of standard deviation $0.03$ and independent feature dropout at
probability $0.10$, which leaves untouched both the direct coverage inputs to
the novelty heads and the entropy input to the fusion gate.

\paragraph{Inputs and initialization.}
The input adapter reconstructs pair probabilities missing from a ranking
record from the available coverage label and score; pair labels and scores
also appear in the pair text input. The two facet embeddings entering
$\boldsymbol{z}_G$ have dimension $32$ each. The model uses DeBERTa-v3-base
with hidden dimension $768$, maximum pair-input length $384$, $K=5$ and $M=8$;
the residual encoder is initialized from the learned joint model, and upstream
checkpoints stay fixed. Padded priors have zero support, support values are
capped at $0.995$, and an empty evidence set falls back to a target-containing
input with all prior support masked out. Training uses refinement weights
$(\alpha_D,\alpha_O,\alpha_{MH})=(0.40,0.30,0.30)$ and mixture weights
$(\lambda_D,\lambda_O,\lambda_R)=(0.50,0.35,0.15)$; development-set
calibration selects $(0.60,0.20,0.20)$ and $\theta_{MH}=0.32$ for inference,
fixed thereafter. The reported checkpoint was selected on development data at
epoch 5 of 8.
